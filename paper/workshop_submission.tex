\begin{abstract}
Code models perform strongly on programming tasks, but it is unclear whether this behavior relies on program semantics or on lexical cues correlated with functionality, such as identifier spelling, token distance, and indentation. As these models are increasingly used for security-relevant tasks such as program analysis, it is important to identify the latent representations on which they rely and the conditions under which those representations fail. We separate three questions: whether a program relation is represented beyond the surface form, whether that representation is robust to changes in form, and whether the model's downstream computation causally uses it. Our main construction pairs Python programs that differ by one binding-changing character while preserving the probing sites, their local token windows, and their distance. On these pairs, a measured surface baseline and a context-free embedding probe both score exactly $0.500$. In DeepSeek-Coder 1.3B and 6.7B, binding rises from this floor to $0.984$ in middle layers and is partly shed before the output; def--use structure follows the same profile. Frozen probes show that the representation is robust to distance and renaming but fragile under semantic interference. At 500 added tokens, inert prose retains $0.921$ binding accuracy while scope-shadowing context reduces it to $0.570$, near chance; control-flow flattening reduces the best obfuscated-code layer to about $0.750$. Finally, we introduce a crossed binding-interchange test designed to distinguish the presence of a latent binding from its causal influence on the answer. \TODOresult{} The completed results therefore characterize not only whether semantic relations are recoverable, but also which transformations preserve them and whether the model uses them in its own computation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Code models produce convincing programs, but it does not necessarily follow that they infer meaning and underlying functionality. Equal names usually denote the same variable, definitions and uses tend to be nearby, and indentation often exposes control structure. A probe that recovers binding or data flow from hidden states may therefore recover a relation computed by the model, or a surface regularity retained in those states.

We call a program property \emph{semantic} to the extent that a stated, bounded reader of surface form cannot recover it. This definition covers two levels used in this work. \emph{Abstract semantics} are sound approximations of behavior, including reaching definitions, def--use edges, control dependence, and taint. \emph{Concrete semantics} describe what a program computes at run time. A claim about one level does not automatically imply a claim about the other: our probes ask whether the model tracks which definition reaches a use, whereas the causal experiment asks whether changing the selected definition changes the value produced by the model.

We enforce the distinction between surface form and semantics rather than estimate it after the fact. Each context-matched pair contains two Python programs that differ only in one identifier character at an inner definition. This mutation changes whether a later, lexically identical use resolves to an outer or inner definition. The two probe anchors remain at identical token indices, the $\pm3$-token windows around them are unchanged, and their distance is unchanged; the semantic label alone flips. A classifier restricted to these surface features must therefore assign the same prediction to both members and scores exactly $0.500$ on the balanced pairs. The context-free embedding probe has the same floor. Performance above it requires information introduced by contextual computation. The word \emph{surface} is nevertheless relative to this stated reader: the construction does not rule out every computable feature of the complete source.

This construction lets us ask three questions that require different evidence. \emph{Representation} asks whether a relation is recoverable beyond controlled surface cues. \emph{Robustness} asks whether a frozen representation survives changes in form and fails selectively when the relational problem becomes harder. \emph{Causal use} asks whether changing the internal relation makes behavior follow the corresponding semantic counterfactual, rather than merely changing the answer. Keeping these questions separate is central: decodability is not causality, and invariance strengthens a representation claim without establishing downstream use.

Structural probing studies recover token, AST, identifier, namespace, data-flow, and other program properties from code-model representations \citep{wan2022capture,hernandez2022astprobe,troshin2022probing,ma2024unveiling}; related work studies latent execution state in models trained on programs \citep{jin2024emergent}. These approaches establish recoverability, whereas black-box behavioral evaluations establish task performance. Neither by itself shows that a recovered relation exceeds the information available to a controlled surface reader or that the model causally uses it. Probe controls are therefore necessary to separate properties of the representation from properties learned by the probe \citep{hewitt2019control}. We combine controlled linear probing with frozen-distribution tests and interchange interventions \citep{geiger2023alignments,wu2023boundless,huang2024ravel}.

Our results support three conclusions. First, binding and def--use are built rapidly with depth and peak in middle layers. Second, the robustness boundary is selective: middle-layer representations survive changes to identifier names and inert distance, but degrade when added context creates competing scopes or when structured control flow is flattened. \section{Framework and Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $x$ be a program, $s(x)$ a semantic relation, and $h_l(x)$ its hidden state at layer $l$. The three dimensions can then be stated as three different relations between program, state, and output.

\paragraph{Representation.}
Here $g$ is the pairwise linear probe used throughout the representation experiments. We say that the model \emph{represents} the relation operationally when this probe recovers $s(x)$ from $h_l(x)$ above a surface-only baseline on the same controlled examples:
\begin{equation}
  \exists g \quad \text{such that} \quad g(h_l(x)) \approx s(x),
  \qquad\text{or abstractly}\qquad
  I\!\left(s(x);h_l(x)\right)>0.
  \label{eq:representation}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:representation} is a claim about information accessible from latent space, not human-like understanding or causal use. The mutual-information expression states the theoretical target; controlled linear probing is its operational test here.

\paragraph{Robustness.}
For a meaning-preserving transformation $T$, the relation stays fixed while the program's form changes. A decoder fitted on the original distribution should therefore remain valid:
\begin{equation}
  s(T(x))=s(x),
  \qquad
  g(h_l(T(x)))\approx s(x).
  \label{eq:robustness}
\end{equation}
We summarize the cost of a transformation by
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{rob}}(T)
  =\operatorname{Perf}\!\left(g(h_l(x))\right)
  -\operatorname{Perf}\!\left(g(h_l(T(x)))\right),
  \label{eq:robustness-drop}
\end{equation}
where \(\operatorname{Perf}\) is accuracy on the relevant controlled stratum. A small drop under surface change and a larger drop under genuine relational interference reveal what the readout depends on.

\paragraph{Causal use.}
Let $y(x)$ be the model output and $\mathcal{I}(h_l(x),s')$ an intervention intended to install alternative semantic state $s'$. Causal use requires the intervened output to follow the semantic counterfactual:
\begin{equation}
  y\!\left(\mathcal{I}(h_l(x),s')\right)
  \approx
  y\!\left(x\ \text{with}\ s\leftarrow s'\right).
  \label{eq:causal-use}
\end{equation}
In this paper, the counterfactual changes which definition is in scope while reversing the values assigned to the two candidate definitions in a held-out arm. A genuine binding intervention must therefore make the output follow the newly selected definition in both assignments. This is stronger than requiring $y(\mathcal{I}(h_l(x),s'))\neq y(x)$: a generic answer-steering edit can change the output, or push toward one fixed token, without transporting which definition is selected.

We study the base, non-instruction-tuned DeepSeek-Coder models. The 6.7B model supplies the main results because it reliably performs the behavioral tasks used in the intervention; the 1.3B model tests whether the representational layer profile replicates at a smaller scale within the same model family. Both models remain frozen. Synthetic Python generators record exact binding and program-graph structure by construction. We map AST source spans to tokenizer positions and verify that each aligned token matches the original source. Def--use labels are independently checked with Beniget, an external Python static-analysis library that computes definition--use relations; agreement on straight-line code and inclusion under branching provide a check that the repository's own graph extractor is not generating self-consistent but incorrect labels. We additionally evaluate 200 AST-parseable Python functions from CodeSearchNet \citep{husain2019codesearchnet}. Obfuscated variants are executed and retained only when observationally equivalent to their source.

For positions $i,j$, pairwise linear probes use
\begin{equation}
 [h_i;h_j;h_i-h_j;|h_i-h_j|].
\end{equation}
Five-fold cross-validation is grouped by source program. This prevents rows that share token states or program-specific templates from appearing in both training and test folds. We retrain the identical classifier after shuffling labels within each program and report \emph{selectivity}, the true-label accuracy minus shuffled-label accuracy. This control asks whether the probe uses the intended correspondence between hidden state and relation, rather than class balance or regularities specific to a program. Negative pairs are reported by difficulty rather than pooled, because a large set of differently named negatives would otherwise dominate the score while being solvable from spelling alone. The decisive \texttt{context\_matched} stratum keeps both counterfactual programs in the same cross-validation group. Its model-free surface baseline sees only $\pm3$ token-ID windows around both anchors and their bucketed distance. It scores $0.500$ by construction on this stratum. We do not claim that this floor covers every computable feature of the complete source.

The corresponding evidential standards are a context-matched surface and embedding floor for representation, frozen readouts under matched or execution-verified transformations for robustness, and a whole-state ceiling plus a held-out answer-direction falsification for causal use. Confidence intervals for intervention effects use paired cluster bootstraps over base programs.

\section{Representation}
\label{sec:representation}

\paragraph{Variable binding is built with depth.}
Figure~\ref{fig:binding} is the central measurement. On context-matched pairs, the surface baseline and embedding probe are both $0.500$ for both models. The first transformer block reaches $0.570$ in 1.3B and $0.531$ in 6.7B; layer 3 reaches $0.961$ and $0.914$. Accuracy peaks at $0.984$ at layer 7 in 1.3B and across layers 11--15 in 6.7B, before falling to $0.930$ and $0.914$ at the final layer. Binding is therefore not linearly recoverable above the measured floor at the input, appears rapidly under contextual computation, and is partly shed near the output. The shared shape across scale is more informative than a small difference at any one layer: both models construct the relation at the same relative depth, while the larger model sustains its peak for longer.

\paragraph{Def--use extends the result from reference to data flow.}
A def--use edge links a definition to a later occurrence that reads its value. Distance-matched negatives remove the shortcut that related tokens are simply closer together. The layerwise profile closely follows binding and peaks near $0.99$ in middle layers. Even the hardest bucket, whose anchors are 50--200 tokens apart, reaches approximately $0.96$--$0.99$. The model therefore preserves directed data-flow structure over meaningful distance, motivating the more controlled distance tests below.

\paragraph{Not every decodable relation is equally semantic.}
% Why this is a contrast: a probe can decode a real program relation without
% showing that the model computed it, when the same labels are already visible
% to a model-free reader. Control dependence is the empirical example of that
% distinction; binding is stronger because its controlled surface floor is chance.
Control dependence asks whether execution of a statement depends on a particular guard. It is highly decodable from hidden states, but the model-free surface baseline already reaches $0.927$ accuracy and $0.990$ AUC. This happens because the controlling guard is often the nearest enclosing \texttt{if}, which is exposed by nearby tokens, indentation, and distance. The hidden-state result may improve on that baseline, but most of the label is already visible without running the model. We therefore use control dependence as a contrast showing that high probe accuracy alone is insufficient: binding provides stronger semantic evidence because its controlled surface reader is fixed at chance. On 200 CodeSearchNet functions, the 6.7B model reaches peak AUC $0.978$ for binding and $0.979$ for def--use, compared with surface baselines of $0.673$ and $0.590$. The similar early-middle peak argues against a result caused only by synthetic generator templates. The exact isolation does not transfer, however, because real identifier spelling is informative and embedding AUC is already about $0.96$.

\section{Robustness}
\label{sec:robustness}

Decodability alone does not reveal whether a probe relies on a stable semantic relation or on a feature specific to the training form. We therefore fit each probe once on base programs and evaluate it unchanged on transformed programs. Because the decision boundary is fixed, a performance change cannot be explained by adapting the probe to the new distribution; it measures whether the original state--relation correspondence survives the transformation.

\paragraph{Distance and interference.}
We insert filler between a tracked definition and use, sized by tokenizer length. The conditions range from inert prose through dead code and fresh-name decoys to competing updates and nested scopes that reuse the tracked names. At 500 inserted tokens, 6.7B binding accuracy is $0.921$ under comment prose, $0.794$ under dead code, $0.795$ under lexical decoys, $0.859$ under updates to other variables, and $0.570$ under scope shadowing. At 1,000 tokens scope shadowing reaches chance, approximately $0.498$, while the other fillers remain above $0.70$. Under shadowing, the middle layers that normally build binding collapse most strongly, whereas block 0 is comparatively stable. The degradation lands on the contextual computation rather than on a lexical lookup.

\paragraph{Spelling and structure.}
We next apply a cumulative, execution-verified ladder: normalization, consistent renaming of locals, opaque predicates, mixed boolean--arithmetic rewrites, and control-flow flattening. Best-layer 6.7B binding accuracy moves from approximately $1.000$ after normalization to $0.897$ after renaming, $0.857$ after opaque predicates, $0.846$ after arithmetic rewriting, and $0.750$ after flattening. The layerwise renaming result is the more informative one: embedding and block-0 probes fall below chance, to about $0.29$--$0.33$, while layers 7--15 remain at $0.85$--$0.90$. Early states retain lexical identity strongly enough that renaming systematically misleads the frozen decoder; middle states support a more abstract relation. Flattening damages even the best layer, showing that the representation is not invariant to arbitrary semantics-preserving compilation.

Together, the two tests locate the representation between lexical matching and syntax-independent program analysis. Its survival under renaming and long inert spans shows that the middle-layer signal is not tied to a particular identifier or token distance. Its failure under scope shadowing and control-flow flattening shows that this signal still depends on how scope and control are expressed in the source. The model therefore forms a relational abstraction, but not one that is invariant to every semantics-preserving rewrite.

\section{Causal Use}
\label{sec:causal}

Everything above is correlational. A faithful representation can coexist with the computation that determines the output without being read by it. Causal use requires more than showing that an intervention changes an answer: the answer must change as the counterfactual semantic relation predicts.

\paragraph{A crossed binding interchange.}
To test causal use, we cross two factors: whether the marked use resolves to the outer or inner definition, and which of two values is assigned to those definitions. This produces four programs per base. In arm \texttt{ab}, the outer definition holds $a$ and the inner definition holds $b$, so changing the binding from outer to inner should change the answer from $a$ to $b$. In held-out arm \texttt{ba}, the assignments are reversed, so the same binding change must instead move the answer from $b$ to $a$. Within each arm, only one upstream identifier character changes; the use token, its position, and total sequence length remain identical. The task also contains no arithmetic: the function returns the variable selected by the binding.

We learn a low-rank interchange on arm \texttt{ab} only. The intervention replaces the host's coordinates in the learned subspace with the corresponding donor coordinates at the marked use, leaving the orthogonal remainder of the hidden state unchanged. If that subspace represents which definition is in scope, installing the inner-binding donor state should select the inner value under both assignments. It must therefore cause opposite token movements across arms: $a\rightarrow b$ during training and $b\rightarrow a$ when held out. By contrast, an edit that merely encodes the training answer $b$, or a fixed direction toward token $b$, can succeed on \texttt{ab} but should fail on \texttt{ba}. This crossed assignment is the falsification: it tests whether the intervention transports the binding relation rather than a correlated answer feature.

The intervention technique follows learned causal alignment work \citep{geiger2023alignments,wu2023boundless,huang2024ravel}. Our contribution is not a new optimization method, but the combination of a construction-pinned surface floor with a held-out value assignment in a pretrained code model. This distinguishes the test from causal binding studies in entity retrieval and small symbolic models \citep{feng2024binding,wu2025variablebinding}.

The prerequisite gates pass on 6.7B. Execution and an independent scope interpreter agree on 400/400 bases, with every construction invariant satisfied (H0). Behavioral binding resolution is $1.000$ overall and in the weakest factorial cell (H1). Use-site binding decoding is $1.000$ against a measured surface floor of $0.500$ (H2). Whole-state interchange moves the answer in both arms, with logit movements of $+4.781$ and $+4.799$, a flip rate of $0.857$, and exact structural zeros (H3). Together, these gates establish that the corpus is valid, the task is within model capability, the use site contains the controlled relation, and both arms can causally affect the answer under whole-state replacement.

They do not establish that downstream computation reads a low-rank binding representation. H4 must show that low-rank interchange beats norm- and rank-matched controls on the training arm. H5 must show that the same subspace transfers to the held-out arm while the explicit answer direction fails. The first H4--H5 attempt is not interpretable because its answer-direction control was not norm-matched to the learned edit. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The three dimensions give an asymmetric answer. \emph{Representation} is established under the measured controls: binding and def--use begin at a chance-pinned surface and embedding floor, emerge rapidly, and peak in middle layers. \emph{Robustness} is qualified: the same frozen readout survives inert distance and renaming most strongly in those middle layers, but degrades under shadowing and flattened control structure. \emph{Causal use} is unresolved: the use site is decodable and causally potent under whole-state replacement, but the decisive semantic-versus-answer intervention is unfinished.

The layerwise and robustness results are consistent with a progression from lexical to relational to output-oriented formats. This is an interpretation of the curves, not a demonstrated decomposition of the network. The failure under flattening further suggests that the relational format is tied to source-level structure rather than a compiler-like canonical semantics.

The scope of the claim is limited. We study two sizes from one model family, Python, and controlled programs emphasizing lexical scope and data flow. The $0.500$ floor is exact for the stated local-window-plus-distance baseline, not every computable feature of the source. Natural-code transfer preserves the layer signature but not the isolation. Frozen-probe failure can also mean a change of representational basis rather than loss of all semantic information. Finally, whole-state interchange establishes causal potency but not binding specificity.

Interpretability claims become scientific when each stronger term is attached to a stronger falsification. A controlled floor supports representation; frozen transformations identify its basis; and crossed interventions are needed for causal use. On this standard, these code models compute scope-sensitive relations whose invariances are limited and measurable. Whether their own downstream computation uses the binding relation itself is the remaining question.

\section{Detailed Methods}
\label{app:methods}

\paragraph{Data.}
The canonical core corpus contains 200 binding programs, 200 taint programs, and 100 shadowing programs. The context study uses 40 bases crossed with five filler types and target insertions of 0, 50, 100, 200, 500, and 1,000 tokenizer tokens. The cumulative obfuscation ladder uses 40 bases. E13 uses 400 bases and four factorial cells per base. All members of a matched family are kept or dropped together.

\paragraph{Ground truth and alignment.}
Generators record exact AST-derived semantic events. Def--use extraction is checked against Beniget by set inclusion when branches allow multiple reaching definitions and by equality on straight-line programs. This audit caught an error in self-referential updates, where a right-hand-side use was initially linked to the same-line target rather than the preceding definition. AST spans are converted to source offsets and then tokenizer indices, and the recovered token text is checked against the source. The model loader rejects tokenizers that fail an exact code round trip.

\paragraph{Probe fitting.}
All probes are linear with $C=0.1$, at most 2,000 optimization iterations, and at most 20,000 examples per task and layer. Dataset caps remove whole source-program groups, never individual rows. Five-fold \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold} keeps every row from a source program in one fold. For pairwise tasks, control labels are shuffled within programs, preserving each program's label distribution.

\paragraph{Robustness.}
Stage-20 probes are evaluated without retraining. Ground truth is rebuilt from each transformed program. Each obfuscated program is executed and checked for the same observed output as its base. This is an empirical observational-equivalence guarantee for the generated cases, not a proof for all possible inputs.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.}
Intervention intervals use paired cluster bootstraps over base programs. Controls are compared on identical rows. Accuracy is used for balanced controlled strata; AUC is emphasized on natural code because identifiers are genuinely predictive there.

\section{Causal Identification and Pending Result}

E13 enforces the following invariants at generation and checks them independently before model evaluation: all four prompts have the same token length; within each arm exactly one token differs; the use token is identical; the mutation is at least four tokens before the use; values are distinct single tokens; the answer appends one token; no arithmetic operator occurs; and the answers cross between arms. Execution is compared with a scope-aware AST interpreter implementing Python's rule that a name assigned anywhere in a function body is local throughout that body.

For a host state and donor state from the alternative binding in the same arm, the outcome metric is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{LD}} =
\left[\log p(y_{\mathrm{installed}})-\log p(y_{\mathrm{own}})\right]_{\mathrm{patched}}
-
\left[\log p(y_{\mathrm{installed}})-\log p(y_{\mathrm{own}})\right]_{\mathrm{clean}}.
\end{equation}
A positive value means that the output moved toward the value selected by the installed binding. The token identity of that value reverses between arms.

Controls include whole-state replacement, an explicit answer direction, rank-matched random subspaces, norm-matched random edits, a no-op, and a pre-mutation structural-zero site. H4 requires low-rank interchange to clear the matched controls on the training arm. H5 requires the same learned subspace to transfer without refitting to the held-out arm, while the explicit training-arm answer direction fails there.

\section{Retired Analyses and Lessons}

We retain failed designs because they determine which causal claims are interpretable. Raw activation patching shows a migration of causal influence from an early changed-token site to the final readout position, but cannot isolate a semantic variable. A J-space coordinate swap near the output changes logits relative to matched random controls, but fails its registered specificity criterion; the use-site null is retracted because the site's response to small edits is strongly nonlinear. A latent-store design is parked because its arithmetic behavioral prerequisite fails. Behavioral lead-time and control-dependence lens claims are retired because their floors or positive controls do not support the proposed absence claims. The common lesson is that a null requires a positive control matched in kind, site, and scale.
