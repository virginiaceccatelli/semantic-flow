\begin{abstract}
Code language models can often produce plausible programs without necessarily tracking the relations that determine what the code means. Identifier spelling, token proximity, and indentation all correlate with semantic structure, so behavioral success and high probing accuracy may arise from surface cues alone. We study whether pretrained code models internally represent variable binding, def--use structure, control dependence, and taint; how these representations change across layers and controlled transformations; whether they affect model behavior; and whether they are aligned with the model's output vocabulary. On context-matched binding pairs, for which both a model-free surface baseline and the embedding layer are exactly at chance, linear-probe accuracy rises from 0.500 to approximately 0.98--0.99 in the middle layers of DeepSeek-Coder 1.3B and 6.7B. Def--use edges show the same profile and remain accurately decodable over 50--200-token distances. Frozen probes survive inert context and several semantics-preserving rewrites, but degrade under scope shadowing and control-flow flattening. Activation patching shows a causal signal moving from the sink argument in early layers to the final-token position in late layers. However, a validated Jacobian lens finds no control-dependence signal where a supervised probe reaches AUC 0.999. After adding appropriate behavioral and statistical floors, we also find no evidence that probe failure precedes model failure. The results support a model in which code LMs construct and sometimes use program-semantic information without maintaining it in a fully invariant or consistently output-aligned form.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Many programming tasks depend on relations that are not visible from a token in isolation. A use of \texttt{x} must be linked to the definition that reaches it. A value passed to a sink may or may not have passed through a sanitizer. A statement may be controlled by one guard rather than another at the same indentation depth. A code model that tracks these relations should behave differently from one that relies mainly on spelling, proximity, or common formatting patterns.

Distinguishing these possibilities is difficult because surface form and program semantics are strongly correlated in ordinary code. Equal names often denote the same variable, definitions and uses are often close, and indentation reveals control structure. Natural identifiers such as \texttt{result}, \texttt{cache}, and \texttt{sanitized} also reveal likely roles. A classifier can therefore recover a semantic label without showing that the model computed the corresponding relation.

Prior work has shown that pretrained code models preserve syntax trees and expose information about identifiers, data flow, control flow, and data dependence \cite{wan2022capture,hernandez2022astprobe,troshin2022probing,ma2024unveiling}. Controlled synthetic studies further show that next-token models can develop representations of latent program execution state \cite{jin2024emergent}. These results establish recoverability, but leave several properties separate: whether a readout relies on a surface shortcut, whether it survives controlled changes, whether the model uses the information, and whether the information is aligned with what the output head can express.

We study these properties in a single pipeline. We generate Python programs with exact binding, def--use, control-dependence, and taint labels and map source spans to verified token positions. Linear probes are evaluated using source-grouped cross-validation, within-program shuffled-label controls, and task-specific hard negatives. Most importantly, context-matched binding pairs hold the visible anchor context fixed while changing the binding relation, forcing both a surface classifier and the context-free embedding layer to chance. Def--use negatives are matched by token distance, and control-dependence negatives occur under sibling guards at the same indentation depth.

We then apply frozen probes to programs with up to 1,000 tokens of inert or interfering filler and to an execution-verified obfuscation ladder. This distinguishes raw length from semantic interference and early name-dependent representations from more structural middle-layer states. We test transfer on 200 CodeSearchNet functions. Finally, activation patching measures causal use, a Jacobian lens measures output-aligned token disposition, and a prefix-by-prefix taint task tests whether internal failure precedes behavioral failure.

Our strongest result is controlled variable binding. Accuracy rises from exactly 0.500 at the surface and embedding floors to approximately 0.98--0.99 in the middle layers of both models. Def--use structure follows the same profile and remains accurate across substantial distance. These representations survive inert length and identifier renaming in the middle layers, but fail under scope shadowing and flattened control flow. Activation patching shows that information associated with a tainted sink argument causally affects the answer and moves toward the output position across depth. Yet decodability does not imply output accessibility: control dependence is nearly perfectly decoded by a trained probe but remains at chance under a validated Jacobian lens.

We do not interpret these results as evidence that the models execute programs symbolically or maintain compiler-like internal data structures. They show that specific program relations are linearly accessible, stable under some transformations, and causally consequential in one task. They also expose limits: the representations are not invariant to all semantics-preserving rewrites, the real-code evaluation cannot fully separate lexical and semantic information, and the corrected behavioral experiment provides no evidence of latent failure preceding output failure.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Structural probing of code models.}
Wan et al. analyze CodeBERT and GraphCodeBERT through attention, probing, and syntax-tree induction, finding that intermediate representations preserve substantial syntactic structure \cite{wan2022capture}. AST-Probe learns a low-dimensional syntactic subspace from which a complete abstract syntax tree can be recovered, with the strongest information in middle layers across five code models \cite{hernandez2022astprobe}. These studies motivate our layerwise analysis, but focus on grammar rather than relations whose labels can change while the visible anchor tokens remain fixed.

Broader probing suites move toward semantic structure. Troshin and Chirkova study syntax, identifiers, namespaces, data flow, and naming across code models and training choices \cite{troshin2022probing}. Ma et al. probe seven models for AST, control-flow graph, control-dependence graph, and data-dependence graph reconstruction, finding consistently strong syntax encoding but more variable semantic encoding \cite{ma2024unveiling}. Their DDG and CDG tasks are the closest precedents for our def--use and control-dependence experiments. We add shortcut-specific controls, frozen-probe stress tests, causal interventions, and an output-aligned readout.

Jin and Rinard train a transformer on synthetic grid-world programs and recover unobserved intermediate execution states, using an interventional baseline to separate model representation from probe learning \cite{jin2024emergent}. We make a complementary tradeoff: pretrained Python models instead of a custom-trained DSL model, and static program relations instead of grid-world state. Synthetic counterfactuals provide exact control, while CodeSearchNet tests natural-code transfer. GraphCodeBERT injects data flow directly through graph-guided attention and structure-aware pretraining objectives \cite{guo2021graphcodebert}; we instead ask whether a token-based pretrained model makes def--use information accessible without receiving a graph at evaluation time.

\paragraph{Probe controls and causal evidence.}
Probe accuracy is not self-interpreting. Hewitt and Liang introduce control tasks and selectivity to distinguish information present in a representation from decisions learned by the probe \cite{hewitt2019control}. We use linear probes, shuffled-label selectivity, and controls specific to code: token-identical binding comparisons, equal-name hard negatives, distance-matched def--use negatives, same-depth sibling guards, and source-grouped folds.

Even a controlled probe establishes availability rather than use. Activation patching tests whether replacing an internal state changes behavior, but conclusions depend on the corruption and effect metric \cite{zhang2023patching}. Our clean and corrupted programs are token-aligned and differ only in the variable passed to a sink. We measure normalized recovery of the clean--corrupted logit difference and treat late final-token patches separately because they may act directly on the output.

\paragraph{Internal availability and output alignment.}
Lu et al. show that language models may encode target information in long contexts while failing to use it in the final answer, a ``know but do not tell'' dissociation \cite{lu2024longcontext}. Our context experiment separates inert length from semantic interference, and our taint experiment similarly finds that an internal readout can remain correct when the output fails. We do not, however, find evidence that internal failure reliably precedes behavioral failure.

The Jacobian lens maps intermediate activations into output-token directions using an average Jacobian from the current layer to the final residual state. Gurnee et al. use it to identify representations available for verbal report and other workspace-like functions \cite{gurnee2026workspace}. We validate the method on code next-token prediction, then test whether a probe-decodable relation is also expressed in an output-aligned direction. Our claim is restricted to this token-level operationalization of verbalizability.

The contribution is therefore not the first observation that code models contain syntax or semantics. It is the joint separation of surface recoverability, contextual construction, robustness, natural-code transfer, causal use, and output alignment.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Models.}
We study frozen DeepSeek-Coder models at 1.3B and 6.7B parameters. The models contain 24 and 32 transformer blocks, respectively. We record the embedding state (layer $-1$), block 0, layers spaced through the network, and the final block. The 1.3B layers are $-1,0,3,7,11,15,19,23$; the 6.7B model additionally includes layers 27 and 31. Cross-model comparisons emphasize relative depth.

\paragraph{Data and ground truth.}
The synthetic core contains 740 Python programs covering binding, taint propagation, shadowing, and matched control dependence. Binding identity and reaching definitions are derived from the AST and construction metadata. Taint programs follow a source--propagation--optional-sanitizer--sink pattern and include the live state after each relevant line. Control dependence is derived from lexical nesting and exact join points.

The context set contains 1,200 variants from 40 bases, with comment prose, dead code, lexical decoys, competing updates, or scope shadowing inserted at sizes from 0 to 1,000 tokenizer tokens. The obfuscation set contains 40 bases at five cumulative levels: normalization, identifier renaming, opaque dead branches, mixed Boolean-arithmetic encoding, and control-flow flattening. Every retained variant is execution-verified, and all levels of a base are kept or rejected together. The causal set contains 40 token-aligned clean/corrupted pairs that differ only in whether a sanitized or raw variable is passed to a sink. Natural-code transfer uses 200 AST-parseable Python functions from CodeSearchNet \cite{husain2019codesearchnet}.

AST character spans are aligned to tokenizer offsets and checked against the source. The tokenizer must exactly reconstruct each program. Def--use extraction is independently cross-checked against Beniget. This check previously exposed a labeling error in a self-referential assignment, motivating independent validation rather than internal consistency alone.

\paragraph{Probes and controls.}
We train linear logistic probes with $C=0.1$, at most 2,000 iterations, and no more than 20,000 samples per task and layer. Five-fold cross-validation is grouped by source program. We report accuracy, ROC AUC, and selectivity, defined as target-label accuracy minus accuracy after labels are shuffled within each source example \cite{hewitt2019control}.

Each task has a shortcut-specific control. Context-matched binding examples fix the visible anchors while flipping the relation, placing both the surface baseline and embedding layer at exactly 0.500. Same-name/different-binding cases make lexical identity predict the wrong label. Def--use negatives match the positive token-distance distribution. Control-dependence negatives lie under a different sibling guard at the same indentation depth. A model-free classifier using local token features and distance provides an explicit surface floor.

For context and obfuscation, probes trained on the core set are frozen and applied without retraining. Ground truth is rebuilt for every variant. This tests stability of the accessible representation rather than learnability in each new condition.

\paragraph{Behavioral and causal tests.}
The taint behavior experiment grows a program one line at a time. At each prefix, a frozen probe predicts the live state and the model answers a forced-choice question using \texttt{yes}/\texttt{no} log probabilities. Probe thresholds are calibrated on held-out examples. We require a usable behavioral signal under balanced accuracy. Early-warning rates are compared with an analytic null determined by each readout's per-prefix error rate, a norm-matched random direction, and a position-only classifier.

For activation patching, we replace the corrupted residual vector with the corresponding clean vector at the sink argument, sanitizer definition, or final token. We report normalized recovery of the clean--corrupted answer-logit difference. For the Jacobian lens, we first require exact agreement with the logit lens at the final layer, where the Jacobian is the identity, and above-random held-out next-token recovery. We then test taint and control-dependence readouts.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Semantic relations emerge after the embedding layer}
\label{sec:representation}

Figure~\ref{fig:binding} shows the central result. On context-matched binding pairs, both the model-free surface baseline and the embedding layer score exactly 0.500. In the 1.3B model, accuracy rises to 0.557 at block 0, 0.962 at layer 3, and 0.981 at layer 7 before declining to 0.915 at the final layer. In 6.7B, it rises from 0.528 at block 0 to 0.906 at layer 3 and 0.991 at layer 11, ending at 0.934. Because the input-level controls are exactly at chance, this rise reflects information constructed through contextual processing rather than recovered from isolated token identity.

The same-name/different-binding stratum makes the transition more visible. Embedding accuracy is approximately 0.001--0.002 because equal spelling strongly favors the wrong relation. By layer 3 it reaches 0.987 in 1.3B and 0.967 in 6.7B. Aggregate binding AUC reaches 0.997 and 0.998, with selectivity around 0.39--0.40 over 423 source groups.

Def--use edges follow the same profile. Peak accuracy is 0.989 for 1.3B and 0.990 for 6.7B, with AUC 0.999 in both models. At layer 3, 6.7B accuracy is 0.995 for edges separated by 0--10 tokens, 0.989 for 10--50 tokens, and 0.985 for 50--200 tokens. At the final layer these values remain 0.984, 0.982, and 0.962. The mild decay rules out a simple adjacency account.

Lexical token type provides the expected contrast, reaching 1.000 accuracy at or near the embedding layer with high selectivity. The joint pattern is straightforward: lexical information is immediately available; relational information is built in early blocks, retained through the middle, and partially reorganized near the output.

Control dependence is decodable but less cleanly separated from syntax. An original task version was invalid because indentation alone gave a perfect surface baseline. With sibling-guard negatives at the same depth, the surface baseline still reaches accuracy 0.927 and AUC 0.990. Its positive and hard-negative recalls are 0.959 and 0.676. Hidden states improve both simultaneously: the 1.3B probe reaches 0.981 and 0.873 with AUC 0.997, while 6.7B reaches 0.995 and 0.923 with AUC approximately 0.999. Thus the hidden state adds genuine relational information, but local syntax already predicts most of the task. This makes control dependence a useful contrast to binding rather than an equally strong isolation result.

\subsection{Robustness reveals a context-sensitive structural representation}
\label{sec:robustness}

Frozen probes distinguish raw length from semantic interference. For 6.7B binding at 500 added tokens, accuracy is 0.921 with prose comments, 0.794 with dead code, 0.795 with lexical decoys, 0.859 with competing updates, and 0.570 with scope shadowing. At 1,000 tokens, scope-shadow binding falls to 0.498 and def--use to approximately 0.59, while all other fillers remain above roughly 0.70. Long inert context is therefore much less damaging than reuse of the tracked names in competing scopes.

The obfuscation results in Figure~\ref{fig:robustness} show the same boundary from another direction. For 6.7B binding, layer-averaged accuracy is 0.974 after normalization, 0.726 after renaming, 0.722 after opaque branches, 0.738 after arithmetic encoding, and 0.590 after flattening. Renaming pushes the embedding and earliest layers below chance, to about 0.29--0.33, but middle layers retain about 0.85--0.90; the best renamed-program result is approximately 0.897 at layer 11. Opaque branches and arithmetic encoding add little damage beyond renaming. Flattening produces the second large break, reducing even the best layer to about 0.75. The 1.3B model follows an almost identical sequence.

The representation is therefore neither a shallow lexical lookup nor a fully invariant symbolic state. Middle layers generalize beyond particular names and tolerate large inert contexts, but remain tied to recognizable scope and control-flow organization.

The same decoders also transfer to natural code. On 200 CodeSearchNet functions, 6.7B binding reaches 0.902 accuracy and 0.978 AUC at layer 7, compared with surface AUC 0.673. Def--use reaches 0.911 accuracy and 0.979 AUC at layer 3, compared with surface AUC 0.590. The 1.3B model peaks at AUC 0.980 and 0.975. Both models reproduce the rise from embeddings to an early-middle peak and an approximately 0.07 decline near the output.

This transfer result does not isolate semantics as cleanly as the synthetic pairs. Natural identifier identity is predictive, so embedding AUC already approaches 0.96. E8 therefore shows that the decoder and layer signature transfer to real Python, not that the transferred signal is independent of lexical cues. Figure~\ref{fig:realcode} is placed in Appendix~\ref{app:figures}; it is useful corroboration but less central than the controlled binding result.

\subsection{Causal use and output accessibility are distinct}
\label{sec:causal}

Activation patching shows a clear movement of causal influence across layer and position. In 6.7B, patching the clean state at the sink argument yields mean recovery 0.985 at layer 0, 0.912 at layer 3, 0.708 at layer 7, and 0.500 at layer 11, then approaches zero near the output. The final-token position follows the opposite trajectory: $-0.007$ at layer 0, 0.145 at layer 11, 0.308 at layer 15, 0.650 at layer 19, and 1.000 at layer 31. The 1.3B model shows the same progression at compressed depth. Patching the sanitizer-definition position yields exactly 0.000 mean recovery at every measured layer in both models.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{patching_recovery_deepseek-coder-6.7b.pdf}
  \caption{Activation-patching recovery in DeepSeek-Coder 6.7B. Causal influence is concentrated at the sink argument early and moves toward the final-token position late. The sanitizer-definition position is inert throughout. Late final-token recovery is reported as a direct readout effect rather than independent evidence of a semantic circuit.}
  \label{fig:patching}
\end{figure}

This establishes a local causal claim. Information at the sink argument affects the answer early, and the effect is transferred toward the output position. It does not prove that the sanitizer is unrepresented elsewhere or identify a complete circuit, because whole-vector patching transports all information at the chosen site.

The Jacobian-lens experiment then asks whether a highly decodable relation is aligned with the model's output directions. The method passes both validation gates. At the final layer, Jacobian- and logit-lens agreement is exactly 1.0000 in both models, as required. On held-out next-token identifiers, top-1 accuracy reaches 0.633 in 1.3B and 0.650 in 6.7B, compared with chance 0.038 and much lower random floors. Before the final layer, the Jacobian correction improves over the logit lens by as much as 0.150 and 0.183, respectively.

At guard anchors, an identity control confirms that the readout is active: in 6.7B, ranking the variable tested by the guard rises from 0.507 at embeddings to 0.813 at layer 31. The control-dependence target remains at chance. The initial pooled analysis was biased late in the network because some negative targets appeared before the guard and were already visible under causal attention. Restricting both candidates to positions after the guard removes this temporal confound. Across 417 matched comparisons per 6.7B layer, accuracy ranges from 0.453 to 0.537 and is 0.506 at the final layer. Chance lies in every program-clustered 95\% interval, with no significant layer after correction. An isolated 1.3B point estimate of 0.576 has interval $[0.495,0.644]$ and does not replicate.

The contrast is sharp: the trained control-dependence probe reaches AUC approximately 0.999, while the validated output-aligned readout remains at chance. Under this token-ranking operationalization, control dependence is decodable but not verbalizable. The result does not imply general inaccessibility because the positive control tests identifier identity rather than another relation, and abstract relations may not correspond cleanly to a single output token.

The taint behavior experiment provides a final distinction. A bare prompt initially made both models constant responders, producing balanced accuracy 0.500 and a spurious scale difference in lead time. After adding few-shot examples and explicitly naming the current variable, the 6.7B behavioral signal reaches accuracy 0.939 and balanced accuracy 0.836 over 342 prefixes. The 1.3B signal remains unusable at balanced accuracy 0.471, so its lead time is undefined.

With the corrected signal, 6.7B provides no evidence of early warning. A no-model position heuristic achieves early-warning excess $+0.113$ over its analytic null, while the trained probe's mean excess is reported as $-0.010$. Across Jacobian, logit, probe, and random readouts, apparent warning is strongly negatively correlated with the fraction of examples on which a readout is never wrong ($r=-0.905$, $p=1.1\times10^{-15}$). Unreliable readouts appear to warn earlier because they make more errors.

The negative temporal result does not make the probe uninformative. At useful layers its per-prefix error is approximately 0.005--0.027, compared with 0.233 for the position floor. At many layers, it remains correct at every prefix for every example on which the model answers incorrectly. The supported conclusion is therefore that internal taint information can remain correct while the output fails, not that internal degradation anticipates failure. The E6 diagnostic figure is included in Appendix~\ref{app:figures}, where the controls can be shown without interrupting the main causal and output-accessibility narrative.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experiments support a simple layerwise account. Lexical information is available at the input. Binding and def--use relations are constructed in the first few blocks, strongly accessible through the middle layers, and partially reorganized near the output. This profile appears in both model sizes and in synthetic and real code. It does not imply a symbolic environment, but it does show that contextual processing creates information unavailable to an input-level surface classifier.

The robustness results specify the abstraction level. Middle layers survive identifier renaming and long inert context, which is inconsistent with a direct spelling or proximity heuristic. Scope shadowing and control-flow flattening nevertheless cause severe degradation. The representation abstracts beyond particular names while remaining dependent on familiar scope and control structure.

Representation, use, and reportability remain separate. Probes establish linear availability. Patching establishes that a sink-argument state can causally affect the answer and that this influence moves toward the output position. The Jacobian-lens result shows that near-perfect supervised decoding need not correspond to an output-aligned direction. The behavioral result adds that an accurate internal readout can remain correct while the model answers incorrectly, without providing advance warning.

One possible interpretation is that the residual stream contains more semantic information than a given output computation consumes. A supervised probe can adapt to any linear coordinates carrying the relation, while the output head accesses only directions prepared for the current prediction. The Jacobian correction accounts for average downstream coordinate change yet still finds no control-dependence signal, suggesting that the null is not explained by a simple rotation into the final basis. However, a different relational output format could produce a different result.

The control-dependence experiments also illustrate why identification strength matters more than peak accuracy alone. Hidden states improve substantially over the hard sibling-guard baseline, but surface AUC is already 0.990. Binding provides a stronger semantic claim because its controlled surface and embedding floors are exactly chance. Probe results should therefore be ranked by the shortcut they exclude, not only by their maximum score.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We study two sizes of one architecture family. The programs are small, single-function Python examples and omit interprocedural flow, object aliasing, exceptions, dynamic dispatch, and concurrency. A planned cross-architecture replication is needed for broader generality.

Linear decodability is not native use. Frozen-probe failure may reflect representational rotation rather than information loss, while successful decoding need not affect ordinary generation. The causal experiment addresses one taint-related contrast only. Its whole-vector intervention transports all information at one position and does not identify a unique semantic direction or complete circuit.

The real-code evaluation demonstrates transfer but not isolated semantic transfer because natural identifiers are predictive. Context-matched mutations of real functions would provide a stronger test. The current tables also omit the size of the real-code same-name/different-binding stratum.

The behavioral result is interpretable only for 6.7B and includes 19 model-wrong examples in the canonical summary. Taint state correlates with prefix depth, explaining the apparent competence of a position baseline. A future corpus should randomize initial taint and include re-taint transitions, and calibration should be repeated across seeds.

Finally, the Jacobian lens uses small single-token candidate sets. Its validation shows that it recovers code-token content, but abstract relations may not correspond to one token direction. We use ``verbalizable'' only to mean detectable as an output-aligned token disposition under this method.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On binding examples where surface features and context-free embeddings are exactly uninformative, both DeepSeek-Coder models construct a representation reaching approximately 0.98--0.99 accuracy in the middle layers. Def--use structure is similarly accessible across substantial distance and transfers to real Python with approximately 0.98 AUC. These states survive inert length and several rewrites but fail under scope shadowing and flattened control flow.

Activation patching shows causal influence moving from a sink argument toward the final-token position across layers. Yet strong decodability does not ensure output accessibility: control dependence remains at chance under a validated Jacobian lens where a supervised probe is nearly perfect. The corrected behavioral experiment likewise finds an internal taint readout that can remain correct when the output fails, but no evidence of early warning.

The resulting picture is not that a code model either understands or does not understand a program in one undifferentiated sense. These models construct program-semantic information, preserve it under some conditions, use selected parts of it, and expose only a subset through their output computation. Each property requires its own controls and interventions.
